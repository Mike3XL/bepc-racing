Surfski downwind simulator

Paddle a surfski through different sea states and learn to read, catch and hold waves. An experiment in whether a simulator can teach, and entertain.

1 · Pick a seaChoose a scenario in the panel, then use the sea-state sliders to make it bigger, smaller or messier.
2 · Pick a paddlerNovice, Advanced or Elite: each has its own boat, power, endurance and stroke.
3 · PaddleSteer onto the face of a wave, power up to catch it, ease off to stay on it. Switch cameras with V.
← → or A DSteer
↑ / WPower up one setting per tap. The settings: −3 −2 −1 brace, 0 glide, 1 2 3 low power, 4 sustained flat-water power, 5 6 7 sprint. Hold to keep climbing. Sprint relaxes back to 4; every lower setting is held.
↓ / SEase off one setting per tap. Three quick taps stop paddling and glide. From the glide each tap braces harder on the wave side (−1 light skim, −2 noticeable drag, −3 heavy; hold to step on). A brace also swings the bow toward that side, and stays until you change it. ↑ eases the brace one level, then resumes paddling (hold ↑ to leave the brace at once).
SpacePause and resume. While paused, drag the timeline to review the run, then resume from any point.
I K J L U OMove the boat 100 m ahead / back / left / right · turn it 30° at once (Shift: 10 times the distance, 3 times the angle)
EEdit the sea (the Sea tab). Esc goes back to the Scene tab.
PShow or hide the side panel.

About this project

A research prototype: the waves, the boat and the paddlers are simplified models tuned to feel plausible, not a certified training device. Works best in Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari on a computer with a graphics chip; on a phone use the on-screen buttons.
