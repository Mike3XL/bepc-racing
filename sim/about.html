Surfski downwind simulator

Paddle a surfski through different sea states in your browser, and see whether a simulator can teach downwind technique, and be fun along the way.

This is an experiment and a work in progress. The sea, the boat and the paddlers are simplified models, tuned to feel plausible. It is not a certified training device, and it will be wrong in places. Corrections and ideas are very welcome.

What it is for

Downwind paddling is hard to learn: the good runs happen in conditions that are hard to find, hard to repeat and not always safe to practise in. The aim here is to find out whether a simulation can reproduce enough of the experience, such as reading the swell, getting the bow lifted onto a face, holding a run and recovering from a bad one, to be useful for learning and for thinking about technique, and to be enjoyable in its own right.

How to use it

  • Pick a sea. The Scenario list in the settings panel holds different sea states, from a gentle 1-ft sea to big following seas, crossing seas, a freighter wake, and modelled stretches of Puget Sound, the Columbia Gorge and Shilshole Bay. Under Sea states, each wave train has a slider that scales it from nothing to double, so you can make any scenario smaller, bigger or messier.
  • Pick a paddler. Novice, Advanced or Elite (see below).
  • Paddle. Space starts and pauses. ← → steer. ↑ adds power: tap for a burst, hold to keep climbing, and your power always relaxes back to your baseline. ↓ eases off: three quick taps stop paddling and glide, and a fourth tap (or holding) puts the blade down in a low brace on the inside of the turn, on the face side, or on your paddling side. ↑ starts paddling again.
  • Look around. V cycles the cameras: a first-person view, a deck view, a chase view, and a Drone that flies beside or behind you (it can carry a second ski, like a paddling buddy, or fly as a plain camera at any height). The map in the corner shows the track and the waves around you, with crests light and troughs dark.
  • Review. Pause with Space, drag the timeline back to look at a run again, and press play to continue from that point. You can mark a stretch and save it as a clip, and load it later to watch it from any camera.
  • Share. The Share button copies a link that opens the same sea, paddler and camera.

The three paddlers

Each paddler type bundles a boat, a power profile, an endurance profile and a paddle stroke, so they race the same sea differently.

Power above critical power drains the reserve; below it the reserve refills. When the reserve is empty you cannot exceed critical power, so a sprint onto a wave costs something. The power meter and reserve bar sit above the timeline.

What is simulated

The sea

The sea surface is a sum of many travelling waves, with sizes, periods and directions that follow the real spectrum of a wind sea, plus swells, chop and, in some scenarios, a ship's wake or a single big set wave. Shorelines, shallowing water and a current change the waves where the scenario has them. Foam and spray are painted from wave steepness and height, with foam placed on the back of each wave; there is no true wave breaking.

The boat

The ski floats. Its hull is a row of slices, each with its own buoyancy, so the bow lifts over a short chop, buries when driven into a steep face too fast, and can pearl. Water piled on the bow deck slows the boat and tips the nose down. The seat bucket stays dry. Forward speed comes from your paddling power against the boat's drag, and the slope of the wave face pushes the boat when you are on it. Broaching is built in: when the wave pushes the stern around, the ski turns broadside.

The paddler

The stroke is animated from measured stroke geometry for the three paddler types: trunk rotation that is largest at the shoulders and smaller at the belly, reach, and leg drive. Idle and low-brace postures blend smoothly with the stroke.

What it is not (yet)

  • It does not model capsizing, getting back on the ski, or swimming.
  • The hull shape is one V10-style design for every paddler, with the boat differences carried by mass, drag and steering.
  • Wave breaking, wind on the paddler and the boat, and the flow around the hull are approximations, so the finest effects (for example exactly how deep a bow buries at a given speed) are tuned to match the experience of paddlers rather than computed.
  • The numbers for the paddler types are estimates chosen to feel right, checked against typical flat-water speeds and race pace.
